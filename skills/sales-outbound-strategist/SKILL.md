# Sales Outbound Strategist
Outbound is research wrapped in conversation. Most "cold outreach fails" because the research was zero and the conversation was a pitch.

## The four-stage outbound system

### 1. List (the precision step — 80% of the outcome)
- **Define the perfect-fit customer** by 3-5 specific attributes (industry, size, tech stack, observable signal that they need you).
- **Build the list manually for the first 100.** Tools (Apollo, Clay, ZoomInfo) come later, after you know what "perfect" looks like.
- **Add a SIGNAL** — recent funding, new hire, tech-stack change, public pain point, content they shipped. Generic lists = generic replies.
- **Tier the list:** 50 dream-fit + 200 strong-fit + 500 plausible. Different sequences per tier.

### 2. Channel mix
Pick 2-3 channels per prospect. Layer them.

| Channel | Strength | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| **Email** | Scale + searchable | Direct, formal, decision-makers |
| **LinkedIn DM** | Personal feel | After warming with comments on their content |
| **LinkedIn voice note** | Differentiated | Mid-funnel, after first reply |
| **Twitter DM** | Founder-to-founder | If they're active there; less for traditional B2B |
| **Phone** | Highest commitment signal | After 2-3 written touches |
| **Video message (Loom/Bonjoro)** | Wow factor | High-value prospects only — doesn't scale |

### 3. Message sequence
A 4-7 touch sequence over 2-3 weeks. Each touch = different angle, never the same pitch reframed.

```
Touch 1 (day 0):  Specific observation about THEM + one-line offer. <80 words.
Touch 2 (day 3):  Different angle — case study, contrarian take, or specific question.
Touch 3 (day 7):  Value-give — useful link, intro, observation. No ask.
Touch 4 (day 12): Soft close — "Worth a chat?"
Touch 5 (day 18): Pattern interrupt — short, casual, last attempt.
Touch 6 (day 30): Breakup — "Sounds like timing's off; reaching out in 3 months."
Touch 7 (day 90): Re-engage with new context (their company news / your new case study).
```

### 4. Reply handling
- Reply within 4 hours during workday. Longer = the warmth dies.
- The first reply is almost never "yes." Treat it as a yes/no question disguised. *"Tell me more"* = curious; *"Not now"* = wrong timing not wrong forever; silence = not enough hook.
- Book the call FAST when interest signals. *"Tuesday 3pm or Wednesday 10am?"* > *"When works for you?"*

## Reply-rate diagnostic
If reply rate < 5%: **list problem**. Audience is wrong.
If reply rate 5-10% but no meetings: **message problem**. Hook works; offer doesn't.
If meetings book but don't close: **fit or pricing problem** (different conversation).
If meetings book and close: **scale the system**. Hire/automate to keep cadence.

## The Katviz-AI-specific play
For an AI-automation agency targeting local service businesses (dental, real estate, legal, fitness, clinics):

1. **Signal** — they have a website with a phone number but no chat / no booking widget / outdated UX. That's the visible "they don't have AI yet" signal.
2. **First touch** — *"Saw [specific observation about their site]. Built something similar for [adjacent local biz] that [specific outcome]. Worth showing you the 90-second demo?"*
3. **Offer** — a 15-min Loom demo of a custom-fit AI receptionist for THEIR specific business (mocked, not real). Removes the "abstract AI" problem.
4. **Close** — Starter package ($1.5K) is the foot-in-door; bigger packages unlock from there.

## Hard rules
- **Personalization or don't send.** A template that mentions their name is still a template. Personalize the OPENER and the OFFER.
- **Send time matters.** Tuesday/Wednesday 9-11am local outperforms; Monday and Friday underperform.
- **No follow-ups longer than the first touch.** Brevity escalates the request, never the pitch.
- **Log everything.** CRM (even Google Sheets) — name, channel, touch number, response. The pattern is in the data, not memory.

## Anti-patterns
- "Saw your profile and would love to connect" — instant delete.
- Multi-paragraph cold emails. <100 words always.
- Calendar links in the FIRST email. Earns nothing; signals "I want time from you" before earning interest.
- "Just following up on my last email" — admits the original failed, doesn't re-pitch.
- Bragging about who you've worked with before establishing what you can do for THEM.
